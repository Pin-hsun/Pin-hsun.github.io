In July 2022, I assisted in a summer therapy workshop at the Taiwan Fund for Children and Families in Hsinchu, working with 12 children with ADHD and histories of abuse under the guidance of Professor Shyh-Heng Wong from the University of Taipei. Together, we designed and facilitated a six-day intensive group therapy program grounded in the principles of object relations theory.

From this experience, I learned the importance of treating children as equal subjects and building genuine closeness with them. Many of these children had experienced oppression within their families, so our goal was to empower them by respecting both their decision to join the group and their opinions about how the group should proceed. We wanted them to feel that they were not being forced into therapy, nor placed under the authority of adults who simply command and punish.

In practice, this meant showing patience—for example, waiting up to twenty minutes for the children to calm down and be ready to participate in each session. When they had emotional outbursts, we listened carefully; when they tried to hurt others, we held them firmly and supportively to ensure both safety and comfort. In this way, we created a therapeutic space that could contain both their love and their hatred, allowing them to express and process the full complexity of their emotions.
